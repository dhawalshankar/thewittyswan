Some stories begin with ambition. Mine began with books, television, and an inexplicable fascination with words.

I have always been an avid reader. Somewhere between turning the pages of books and listening to *Wah Wah Kya Baat Hai*, hosted by the inimitable Shailesh Lodha, I developed an early affection for poetry. The rhythm of words, the beauty of a well-crafted couplet, the magic of making an entire room pause and ponder over a few lines — it all fascinated me.

Before I knew what literary pursuits meant, I was already writing verses, reciting them, and occasionally singing my own compositions during my school days. Poetry, for me, was never merely about rhyming words. It was about finding extraordinary expressions for rather ordinary human experiences.

Years later, at university, I participated in an **Induction Slam Poetry competition and secured the first prize.** It was a rather encouraging moment, a little affirmation that perhaps those years of scribbling verses had amounted to something.

And then came an interesting little coincidence.

The following year, I came across an advertisement in *Amar Ujala* inviting aspiring poets to submit their compositions for publication.

I thought, why not?

I picked a poem, sent it across, and went about my day without particularly expecting anything extraordinary.

The next morning, there it was.

**My poetry, published in Amar Ujala.**

There is something profoundly surreal about seeing words that once existed only in the privacy of your notebooks find their way into the pages of a newspaper read by thousands.

I had spent years reading other people's words, admiring poets, and finding inspiration in their verses. And suddenly, someone somewhere was reading mine.

Perhaps that is the beautiful thing about pursuing something you genuinely love. You never quite know where it might take you.

From listening to Shailesh Lodha on television to winning a university slam poetry competition, and eventually finding a place in the pages of Amar Ujala — it has been quite a journey.

I still write. I still read. And I still believe that a few well-chosen words can accomplish what a thousand ordinary sentences cannot.

**Sometimes, all it takes is a poem, a newspaper advertisement, and the audacity to press Send.**

---

*Somewhere between being a reader of poetry and becoming a published poet, I found another reason to keep writing.*
